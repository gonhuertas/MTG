# The Magic: The Gathering Color Pie — Philosophy of Each Color

A faithful summary of the five "My Words" articles (*Making Magic*, 2025), in which Mark Rosewater lets each color of *Magic* explain its own philosophy in the first person, including how it sees every other color. Each color speaks for itself, so the descriptions of "enemies" are deliberately uncharitable — they are how that color frames its opponents, not a neutral judgment.

## The structure of the color wheel

The five colors sit on a wheel. Each color has **two allies** (its immediate neighbors) and **two enemies** (the two colors across from it). The wheel order is White → Blue → Black → Red → Green → (back to White).

| Color | Allies (neighbors) | Enemies (opposite) | One-line creed |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **White** | Blue, Green | Black, Red | Peace and prosperity through order, morality, and the group. |
| **Blue** | White, Black | Red, Green | Perfection through knowledge, and self-improvement of a "blank slate." |
| **Black** | Blue, Red | White, Green | Power through ruthless self-interest; be your own champion. |
| **Red** | Black, Green | White, Blue | Freedom and fulfillment through emotion, impulse, and action. |
| **Green** | Red, White | Blue, Black | Acceptance of nature as it is; find and fulfill your role. |

The central tension of the wheel is often framed as: White (order/group) vs. Black (self) and Red (chaos); Blue (intellect/nurture) vs. Red (emotion) and Green (instinct/nature). The two great axes are **order vs. chaos** (White–Blue against Black–Red, roughly) and **mind vs. nature** woven through it. Each color's allies share *part* of its outlook; its enemies invert its core value.

## White — Peace through order

### Core philosophy
White looks at a world full of crime, injustice, hunger, disease, and suffering and insists: *it does not have to be this way.* There is enough for everyone; suffering persists because people prioritize themselves over the welfare of others. A utopia with no hunger, no crime, and no fear is genuinely achievable, and it requires only one (very large) change: people must learn to put the **needs of the group ahead of their own personal needs** — to ask "What does the group need?" instead of "What do I want?"

The obstacle is that human motivation is naturally internal and selfish — not out of malice, but because self-interest is the path of least resistance. Empathy exists in everyone but must be *nurtured*. White's solution is **structure**: build a society where helping one another is baked into how it works, using positive incentives (reward, belonging, status) and negative ones (loss of resources, loss of freedom, being shunned).

White's two great tools are:

- **Morality** — teaching an objective right and wrong, often reinforced through religion, so that doing good earns pride and ultimate reward while doing evil earns guilt and punishment. This combats *selfishness*.
- **Law** — a clear, public list of forbidden acts with proportional punishments, plus a judicial system to weigh circumstances. This combats *recklessness*.

White names **selfishness and recklessness** as the two greatest threats to peace. It would ideally have no enemies and a single world working toward a shared vision, but accepts building smaller communities along the way and hopes its ideals spread by example.

### Strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** Order gives White more answers to more threats than any other color; given time to plan, it can solve almost any problem. It also has clean-conscience morality — it can sleep at night.
- **Weakness:** Its answers are *prescriptive* — tailored to anticipated threats — so an unforeseen threat can catch it flat-footed. Its moral limits (e.g., refusing to kill, preferring imprisonment) sometimes let foes escape and undo its work.

### How White sees the others
- **Blue (ally):** Shares a love of civilization and systems and long-term thinking. But Blue cares more about the civic side than the moral side, is willing to let people "fall through the cracks" to improve the system, and obsesses over optimizing each *individual* — distributing resources "unfairly." White raises the floor (care for all); Blue raises the ceiling (highest potential).
- **Green (ally):** Shares a love of community and the power of the group — Green's "web of life" echoes White's society, and Green cares about the moral/spiritual side. But Green runs on **instinct** and has a "wild side" that scares White, which believes in restraint and rules over impulse.
- **Red (enemy):** "Chaos personified." Red does whatever it wants with no thought for consequences; recklessness is exactly what White's laws exist to stop. White grudgingly admires that Red genuinely cares about personal connections and is capable of self-sacrifice for those it loves.
- **Black (enemy):** "The champion of selfishness," which treats immorality as a trophy and cruelty, disease, and violence as tools. Its end goal is a dystopia where the many suffer so a few thrive — the exact opposite of White's aim. White notes, warily, that Black has learned to co-opt White's own systems (law, religion).

## Blue — Perfection through knowledge

### Core philosophy
Blue believes every person is born a **blank slate** (Aristotle's *tabula rasa*) — free of fixed mental content. Who you become is shaped by the people you meet, the knowledge you gain, the experiences you have, and the tools you can access, but above all by your own internal drive: *you become who you want to be.*

From this follow Blue's priorities:

- **Knowledge is power.** To make good life choices you must arm yourself with information. Education — formal and informal, from books, people, and experience — is the highest priority, pursued as early and as deeply as possible.
- **Deliberation over haste.** Don't rush decisions. Take time to understand every ramification so the choice you make is the best long-term one. (Blue's vacation analogy: research and rank options rather than just driving off at random.)
- **Experience.** Knowing is not the same as doing; lived experience teaches what you enjoy and what you don't.
- **Tools and technology.** They let you do what you otherwise couldn't (you don't give up reading when words blur — you get glasses).

Blue's ideal society values **efficiency over expediency** and **intellect over emotion**, reveres teachers as its central pillar, and builds depositories of knowledge accessible to all so everyone can reach their potential. This optimization scales from the individual up to family, community, and society.

### Strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** Knowledge — Blue simply knows more, can see and understand *systems* others can't (hence it is the master of countering spells and of control), and given enough time can build resources to answer any threat.
- **Weakness:** It *needs time to process.* Blue's magic is reactive; an aggressive or indirect opponent who refuses to fight on Blue's terms can overwhelm it before it is ready.

### How Blue sees the others
- **White (ally):** Shares the love of optimization at the societal level and long-term thinking. But White favors a single strong message and sends everyone down the *same* path, treating every individual the same — which Blue thinks wastes individual potential. (White raises the floor; Blue raises the ceiling.)
- **Black (ally):** The color that most agrees with Blue on **personal choice** — no destiny, you choose your path. Blue focuses on *identity* (who you are), Black on *role* (what you can do); both wield knowledge as a weapon and accept subterfuge. But Black is reckless in its hunger for power and willing to let some shine at others' expense, which Blue is not.
- **Red (enemy):** All emotion, snap decisions, and short-term thinking — "the embodiment of carelessness" that can destroy in seconds what took years to build. Blue does, however, admire Red's creativity: its disconnected state lets it reach tangential ideas Blue can't.
- **Green (enemy):** The ancient **nature vs. nurture** conflict. Green says you're beholden to your genes and must accept the identity you're born with; Blue says you're a blank slate who chooses. Green disdains intellect and artifice, would abolish Blue's tools, and "wallows in ignorance." Blue concedes one point: natural evolution really does let better ideas win, and there's much to learn from it.

## Black — Power through self-interest

### Core philosophy
Black considers itself the **most misunderstood** color. What it actually wants: for every person to have the *opportunity* to thrive. It doesn't expect everyone to succeed — most won't — but those willing to work and dedicate themselves should have the chance.

Black is the **pragmatist**: it sees the world as a harsh place where people are fundamentally selfish, and it doesn't see that as bad. Everyone deserves a champion, and the only person who truly has your best interests at heart is *you.* Its core tenets:

- **Be your own champion.** Look out for yourself first (the "put on your own life vest first" principle). You may help loved ones if it doesn't conflict with your own needs.
- **Merit and a zero-sum world.** Fates rise and fall with accomplishments; gain comes with someone else's loss, and that's acceptable — the winners earned it, the losers weren't as good.
- **Conquer fear.** Most people fail because they won't seize opportunity, paralyzed by fear of failure or of doing what they're "not supposed to." Black distinguishes this from recklessness: it favors *careful, methodical, ruthless* pursuit of excellence.
- **Power** is the goal — the ability to do whatever you want, to live on your own terms. Black's method: (1) decide what you want, (2) list every step to get it, (3) identify the obstacles, (4) inventory every resource you have, (5) use the resources to overcome the obstacles — lather, rinse, repeat — doing whatever it takes.

### Strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** Black sees opportunities and has **no taboos, no qualms, no mercy, no limitations.** It is unattached — willing to sacrifice anything of value to gain something it needs — and willing to take (calculated) risks.
- **Weakness:** Things won't always work out. Its willingness to meddle with forces other colors avoid means some risks backfire badly. With risk comes reward — but also punishment.

### How Black sees the others
- **Blue (ally):** Black "adores" how thoroughly Blue thinks things through; Blue has a plan, calculates every ripple, and wields knowledge as power without falling for White's "morality scam." Criticism: Blue chases the *ideal* over the merely good-enough and won't take necessary risks, which slows it down.
- **Red (ally):** The color of **action** — Red wants something and does something about it, with drive, passion, and no shame about destruction. Criticism: Red is reckless even for Black's taste and short-sighted, doing things one day that cause problems the next.
- **White (enemy):** "A master of convincing people to act against their own interests." Black calls White's "good of the group" a *scam* and morality a *made-up story* used (via fear and guilt) to brand Black a villain. Black grudgingly respects White's gift for organization and has co-opted White's tools — law and religion — for its own ends.
- **Green (enemy):** Black's problem is Green's concept of **destiny** — the claim that some things "weren't meant for you" — and Green's self-serving version of "survival of the fittest" (a bear killing a stag is "nature," but Black killing a rival is "usurping the natural order"). Still, Black respects that Green is the only *other* color that truly appreciates death (though Black thinks Green is scared to use it as a tool) and admires Green's ferocity.

## Red — Fulfillment through emotion and action

### Core philosophy
Red's question is: *what's the purpose of a finite life?* Its answer: **live life to its fullest** — so that on your deathbed you can say you found love, made friends, did exciting things, and have great stories to tell. Red offers a simple two-step recipe:

1. **Listen to your own body.** It knows what you need. Just as hunger tells you to eat and tiredness tells you to rest, loneliness, anger, and sadness are your body telling you it needs something. Don't treat your inner voice as dangerous.
2. **Act on the message.** Inaction is easy and safe but empty. Taking action — telling someone how you feel, trying the thing — may or may not get you what you want, but you're always better off than not trying. Life is messy; embrace the messiness.

What separates the fulfilled from the unfulfilled is the willingness to **act**. Passion is core to feeding the soul — care about something with all your heart, and have as many passions as you have time for. Red admits its philosophy invites conflict (two people may want the same thing) but considers conflict, disagreement, even violence to be "people being people," not a catastrophe. Desire is not evil; it's core to experience. Red would rather live a life of high highs and low lows than feel nothing.

Red also pushes back on its own stereotype: anger is only *one* facet. Love, happiness, sadness, fear, and disgust matter just as much — it's about embracing *all* emotions, not just the ones that blow things up.

### Strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** **Speed.** Red decides fast and acts fast — attacking "before my opponent has finished their coffee" — and combined with its destructive ability, that aggression is a serious threat.
- **Weakness:** **Short-sightedness.** Acting in the moment and considering consequences later gets Red into trouble; it makes more than its share of mistakes (though Red insists mistakes are valuable teachers). "Some might say I'm my own worst enemy."

### How Red sees the others
- **Black (ally):** They see eye to eye — Black gets that it's okay to prioritize your needs, tap your destructive side, take risks, and get your hands dirty, *and* Black is fun. Caveat: Black takes selfishness to an extreme and would "stab their mother in the back," so never fully trust it. Red, by contrast, prizes **loyalty** to the people who matter to it.
- **Green (ally):** Also a color of action over thinking — instinct rather than impulse, but it plays out similarly, and both love breaking and blowing things up. Shared pet peeves: artifacts, flying creatures (and "Ned"). Red doesn't get Green's spiritual, community-minded "web of life" side — when Green calms down, Red finds it boring.
- **White (enemy):** "Loves telling everyone how to live their life," piling on endless rules that Red sees as oppression dressed up as order. White labels passion "selfishness" and Red "anarchy," but Red claims to be the real good guy — the one trying to help people *feel*, experience, and live before they die.
- **Blue (enemy):** "The mastermind of not acting." Blue thinks everything through and is terrified of emotions and mistakes — but Red argues mistakes are the best teacher, and while Blue spends a day analyzing one optimal decision, Red lives a hundred experiences. A book doesn't know you; emotions come from real experience.

## Green — Acceptance of nature

### Core philosophy
Green is the one color **not advocating for change.** The world isn't broken and doesn't need fixing — nature is already a perfect, self-balancing system, and the goal of life is to **accept what is** rather than chase what could be. The other four colors all argue the world is somehow less than it should be; Green's message is to stop striving and start *acknowledging.*

Sit quietly in nature, Green says, and you'll discover four truths: (1) there is a deep sense of **peace** and belonging — you are part of the world, not a stranger to it; (2) **everything has a role** in the ecosystem; (3) everything is **interconnected** — nothing exists in a vacuum, and to understand a creature you must understand its environment; (4) **everything happens for a reason** — Green is not against change, but change in nature is slow, purposeful, and measured in lifetimes (evolution), not minutes.

From acceptance comes Green's path to satisfaction, in four steps: acknowledge that you're part of something vast (nature); discover your **innate skills** (each creature is born with what it needs); through trial and error, find how best to **apply** those skills — your role/destiny; then **fulfill that role and help others find theirs.** Green's pitch is the simplest of all the colors: you don't need to change anything about yourself — you are already perfect; you only need to adjust how you *think* and accept it.

### Strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** **Unwavering belief.** Every other color is searching for something; Green has already found its answers. That certainty grants focus — access to more colors, more mana, and more efficient creatures. "I know what I want, and I am all in."
- **Weakness:** Over-reliance on the things it cares about. When its routines fail — e.g., when it loses its own creatures, which it depends on to answer threats — Green struggles to respond. (Greatest strength pushed too far becomes greatest weakness.)

### How Green sees the others
- **Red (ally):** The other color that looks *internally* for motivation — impulsive rather than instinctual, but similar. Green enjoys Red's carefree, in-the-moment, untamed energy and shares the willingness to let the "wild side" loose. Gripe: Red doesn't get Green's spiritual side, can't sit still or be introspective, and has only a limited sense of community.
- **White (ally):** Shares the value of **community**, faith in the nontangible, and the idea that the group is greater than the sum of its members. Differences: White treats all members the *same*, while Green treats each individual *uniquely*; White leans on **rules, cities, and morality**, while Green relies on natural systems and lets each individual follow its instincts; White's faith is religion, Green's is spirituality.
- **Blue (enemy):** Blue only accepts what it can tangibly prove and "doesn't do faith," but there's no logical proof for why a sunset is beautiful. Green runs on instinct, Blue on intellect. Worse, Blue's blank-slate doctrine denies that **where you come from** means anything. Green grants there's common ground — both like exploring and categorizing — but says Blue looks to the future while Green is enamored with the past.
- **Black (enemy):** Nature is interconnected, which demands **responsibility** — but Black abhors responsibility, dresses selfishness up as ambition, and insists you can only succeed at others' expense. If every part of the web acted purely for itself, the whole ecosystem would collapse. Green also rejects Black's view of **death as a tool** (for Green, death is a natural, important part of the life cycle, and Green does not fear it). Green does credit Black for understanding the cycle of life, appreciating nature's dark side, and never giving up — "if only it were just a little less greedy."

## Cross-checked relationship map

Every pairing below is described *by both colors involved*, and the two accounts agree on whether they are allies or enemies — the wheel is internally consistent.

**Allied pairs (neighbors):**

- **White & Blue** — civilization, systems, planning, long-term optimization. (Tension: group vs. individual; floor vs. ceiling.)
- **White & Green** — community and the power of the group; faith/spirituality. (Tension: same-for-all rules vs. unique-role instinct.)
- **Blue & Black** — personal choice, no destiny, knowledge as a weapon. (Tension: ideal vs. good-enough; risk-aversion vs. risk-taking.)
- **Black & Red** — action, self-interest, willingness to destroy and take risks. (Tension: cold extreme selfishness vs. Red's loyalty; calculated vs. reckless.)
- **Red & Green** — internal motivation, action over thought, embracing the wild/destructive side. (Tension: Red's impulse and limited community vs. Green's instinct and "web of life" spirituality.)

**Enemy pairs (opposites):**

- **White vs. Black** — group/morality/order vs. self/ambition/power. Utopia vs. dystopia.
- **White vs. Red** — order and restraint vs. chaos and impulse.
- **Blue vs. Red** — intellect, deliberation, knowledge vs. emotion, speed, action.
- **Blue vs. Green** — nurture/blank-slate/artifice vs. nature/destiny/instinct (the nature-vs-nurture axis).
- **Black vs. Green** — selfishness and death-as-tool vs. responsibility, the web of life, and destiny.
